Current test rules
Kings move one square in any direction, cannot move into check, and are required for checkmate. Kings may capture undefended enemy pieces, swap places with adjacent friendly pieces, and are immune to rook explosions. Castling follows standard chess rules, subject to the usual clear-path and check restrictions. The board starts with one king per side on e1 and e12, one queen per side on d1 and d12, eight pawns per side on ranks 2 and 11, two knights per side on b1/g1 and b12/g12, two bishops per side, and rooks in the four corners. Four warships begin on the water directly behind those rooks, one behind each rook. Warships move horizontally or vertically like rooks, but only through water squares. Friendly warships block their route; enemy warships can be jumped over but cannot be captured or landed upon. Each warship carries one bomb and may place it on an empty land square along its inward rook-like attack line. A bomb destroys any non-king piece that lands on it; a king safely removes it. When captured, the bomb returns to the warship that placed it. Pawns move one square forward, sideways, or diagonally forward; they may also move two squares forward if both squares are clear. They cannot move backwards or en passant. Reaching the farthest rank lets a pawn promote to a queen, bishop, knight, or rook. Queens move any distance horizontally, vertically, or diagonally, like a standard chess queen. Each queen can deploy up to three mini-queens on squares it could reach with a queen move. Mini-queens move one square in any direction and cannot give check or checkmate. Captured mini-queens return to their owner's reserve; a queen can also collect its own mini-queens by moving onto them. Bishops move diagonally and may ricochet once per move from an outer wall or enemy piece; capturing ends their move. Knights cannot move adjacent. They make a standard L-shaped leap and may repeat that exact leap once in the same direction. Enemies on intermediate squares are captured; landing on an enemy also captures it and ends the move. Rooks move horizontally or vertically and capture every enemy piece along their chosen path. Friendly pieces block their path. When captured, a rook explodes, destroying enemy pieces immediately to its left and right on the same rank, as well as the piece that captured the rook.
